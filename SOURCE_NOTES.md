# Astronomy Source Notes

## Scope

The explorer intentionally compresses and re-spaces physical sizes and distances so every planet can be selected in one working view. The values in its fact cards are physical values, not scene values.

## Primary Data

- NASA NSSDC, [Planetary Fact Sheet - Metric](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/), accessed 2026-09-14. Card values for planetary diameter, solar distance, orbital period, length of day, and mean temperature come from this table. The page was last updated 2025-03-18.
- NASA Science, [About the Planets](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/), accessed 2026-09-14. Used for the eight-planet census and terrestrial, gas-giant, and ice-giant classification language.

## Treatment

- Solar distance is the NASA fact sheet's mean distance from the Sun, expressed in millions of kilometers.
- `Day` is the fact sheet's length of solar day. `Year` is orbital period in Earth days.
- Mean temperatures are NASA fact-sheet values. They are a useful comparison field, not a local forecast or a surface condition for giant planets.
- The Sun and Moon cards use NASA Science overview pages for descriptive copy; compact values are included only where their units remain meaningful in a quick comparison.

## Asset Notes

The local texture maps are existing project assets. Their presentation is improved with sRGB output, ACES filmic tone mapping, and anisotropic texture filtering. This does not assert scientific image provenance for the texture files.
